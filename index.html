How the power score is built

SRS is a rating in points per week, centred on zero, that adjusts every margin for who you played — so the last-place team hammering the first-place team counts for far more than the same margin against a weak side, and losing narrowly to the best team barely hurts. All-play is your record if you'd played every team every week, which removes schedule luck entirely. Luck is how far your real record sits above or below that; positive means the fixture list has been kind.

What the columns mean

Sorted by VOR — value over replacement, i.e. how many points this player beats the waiver-wire alternative at his position by. That's the number that matters, not raw projected points, because every team's quarterback scores a lot.

Grade is what round he's worth, scaled to your league size: S++/S+ are round 1, S is round 2, A+ rounds 3–4, on down to F for undraftable. Tier is different — it marks the cliffs within a position, so players inside one tier are near-interchangeable. Bye is his bye week. SoS rates his schedule 1 (easiest in the league) to 10 (hardest). Val is how far past his average draft position he's still sitting there; positive means he's falling to you.

2. Starting lineup slots

How many of each slot your team starts each week. FLEX = RB/WR/TE, SUPER_FLEX = QB/RB/WR/TE, BN = bench. These drive both the optimizer and the draft board's value math.

Two different schedule measures, deliberately. On the League page, schedule strength means the fantasy teams you have to play, scored by their power rating — that's the one that decides your record. On the Teams page it means the NFL defences a player faces, which is a drafting consideration and feeds the Bye and SoS columns on the board.

The NFL version is a proxy: this API publishes no "fantasy points allowed by position" data, so difficulty is measured by each defence's own projected output — units that collect sacks, interceptions and shutouts are the ones that smother offences. Useful for ordering matchups, not a precise forecast. The schedules and bye weeks themselves are exact, reconstructed from the week-by-week opponent listed on every projection.
